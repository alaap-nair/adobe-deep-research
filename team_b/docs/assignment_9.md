### Behavior / performance observation
Four concrete cross-model differences from this run:

1. **Refusal aggressiveness varies 6×**: `mistral_baseline` refused 6/40 questions while `gemma2_27b` refused only 1/40. With identical retrieval context across models, this reflects each synthesis model's willingness to ground vs. extrapolate. Out-of-corpus questions (q10 nitrogen-fixing bacteria, q20 mitochondria function, q23/q30 CRISPR, q33 plasmid) drive most refusals; gemma's near-zero refusal rate maps directly to its lowest faithfulness score (0.817 vs. ≥0.89 for the other three) — it's answering from pretrained knowledge instead of refusing.
2. **Faithfulness vs. answer-relevancy tradeoff**: `mistral_baseline` and `qwen25_72b` win faithfulness (~0.94) but trail on answer-relevancy (0.64–0.73). `gemma2_27b` and `phi4` are inverted — lower faithfulness, higher relevancy. The system as currently configured forces a choice between "right but terse" (mistral) and "elaborate but occasionally hallucinated" (gemma).
3. **Context precision is the bottleneck**, not the synthesis model**: all four context_precision scores cluster between 0.543 and 0.592, regardless of synthesis model. That's expected — retrieval is identical across models — and confirms the §5 motivation for adding a cross-encoder reranker as the next improvement.
4. **Latency spread of ~3×**: `mistral_baseline` p50=3.7s, `gemma2_27b` p50=5.3s, `phi4` p50=7.9s, `qwen25_72b` p50=15.1s. Qwen's 72B parameter count costs roughly 4× the smallest model in wall time without a meaningful quality lift.

One JSON-schema regression on mistral_baseline q26 ("How does RNA regulate gene expression?"): mistral's original output omitted the `reasoning` field. We added defensive defaults to `qa_client.parse_llm_json` and the QAResponse instantiation to fill in fallback strings, plus a regex-based fallback parser for truncated/malformed JSON; subsequent runs (qwen, gemma, phi-4) had zero schema errors. The fix also caught a separate gemma-2 trailing-comma issue and a phi-4 max-tokens truncation that would have surfaced as crashes without the new tolerant parser.

## 5. Improvement: Cross-Encoder Reranker

**Source**: Nogueira & Cho (2019), [_Passage Re-ranking with BERT_](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04085). Modern open-weights instantiation: `cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2` (~80MB, CPU-fast, MS-MARCO–trained).

**Implementation**:
- New module `src/reranker.py` wrapping `sentence_transformers.CrossEncoder`.
- Hook in `src/query_engine.py:retrieve_context`: when `USE_RERANKER=1`, fetch `top_k_chunks * 3` candidates from Qdrant, score `(query, chunk_text)` pairs with the cross-encoder, keep top `top_k_chunks`.
- Default behavior is unchanged (`USE_RERANKER=0`), so the baseline run is reproducible.

**Why this improvement (vs. other candidates)**: the W3A canonicalization + W3B schema-typed extraction shipped in prior weeks lifted `context_recall` (+0.112) and `faithfulness` (+0.020), but `context_precision` stayed essentially flat (+0.001). That signature — recall up, precision flat — is the textbook signal for adding a cross-encoder rerank stage on the retriever's candidate set.

**Before / after RAGAS** (mistral_baseline as the synthesis model in both runs):

| Metric              | Pre-rerank | Post-rerank | Δ |
|---------------------|-----------|-------------|---|
| faithfulness        | 0.940     | 0.935       | −0.004 |
| answer_relevancy    | 0.644     | 0.698       | **+0.054** |
| context_recall      | 0.742     | 0.723       | −0.019 |
| context_precision   | 0.567     | 0.543       | −0.024 |

Per-question deltas on the 3 manually-scored questions are visible in §4 above.

**Honest finding: the reranker did not deliver the expected precision lift.** Three of four metrics moved very slightly negative; only `answer_relevancy` improved (+0.054). The manual scoring in §4 explains the mechanism: on q3 the chunk set is unchanged, on q6 the cross-encoder pushed the multi-hop bridging chunk (Ch 24 decomposition mechanism) out of the top-5 in favor of a Ch 8 RuBisCO surface-match, and on q10 it surfaced a misleading "soil nitrogen" mycelium chunk.

**Hypothesis on the cause**: the MS-MARCO MiniLM cross-encoder is trained on web-search query/passage pairs, where lexical overlap with named entities is a strong signal. Our biology questions need *cross-chapter* and *mechanism* matches — q6 specifically rewards finding the Ch 24 decomposition chunk for a question that opens with "How does carbon fixed by RuBisCO". The cross-encoder's training distribution disagrees. A biology-domain cross-encoder (e.g., BioBERT-finetuned) or a different reranker family (e.g., ColBERT late-interaction) would be the natural follow-up.

**The improvement still ships**: it is gated by `USE_RERANKER` so the default behavior is unchanged. The harness (`src/reranker.py` + `query_engine.py` hook), the experiment script (`scripts/run_rerank_experiment.sh`), the manual rubric, and the comparison output (this doc) are reusable for the next reranker we try.

## 7. Key Observations (short answer)

- **Biggest improvement**: extending the corpus from photosynthesis+fungi to also cover OpenStax Ch 14 + 15 (DNA/RNA/replication/translation) turned 14 of the 20 incoming genetics questions from guaranteed out-of-corpus refusals into in-corpus answerables, with 240 new typed triples in Neo4j and 205 new chunks in Qdrant. This is the single change that materially expanded what the system can answer.
- **Biggest failure**: the cross-encoder reranker did not move context_precision in the right direction (Δ = −0.024). The MS-MARCO MiniLM model rewards lexical overlap with named entities in the question, which on multi-hop biology questions like q6 pushes Ch 8 RuBisCO chunks above the Ch 24 decomposition chunk that actually closes the answer chain. Manual scoring confirmed the regression at the chunk-content level, not just the aggregate metric.
- **Main bottleneck right now**: the retriever surfaces the right *topic* but not the right *mechanism*. context_recall is healthy across all four synthesis models (0.67–0.74) and faithfulness is high where the model answers (mistral and qwen ~0.94), but context_precision plateaus around 0.55 — so chunks reach the LLM that the LLM itself rates as not directly answer-supporting. The fix is a domain-aware reranker (BioBERT cross-encoder, or ColBERT late-interaction) rather than the generic web-search MiniLM we tried, *plus* widening the candidate pool past 3× top_k so cross-chapter chunks get a fair shot.
